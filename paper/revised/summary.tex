\begin{center}
{\large\textbf{When the Attack Is Not an Attack:}}\\[1pt]
{\large\textbf{Validity Failures in LLM-Generated Red-Teaming}}\\[2pt]
{\small Yihang Jiao \quad$\cdot$\quad yihangj3@illinois.edu \quad$\cdot$\quad
\url{https://github.com/yhj3/counterfactual-textattack}}
\end{center}
\vspace{-2pt}
\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}
\vspace{2pt}

\noindent
Automated red-teaming uses one language model to generate the probes that test
another. A probe only tells us something about the target if it is a valid test of
the target. I measure four ways it fails to be, on 32 configurations and 600 fresh
generations.

\subsection*{Label validity --- overstates by $3.05\times$}

A task-specific rewriting prompt roughly doubles the attacks that survive
(\textbf{119/180} vs.\ 58/180, $p=1.7\times10^{-10}$, dataset and target held fixed).
On sentiment this is free (similarity 0.713 vs.\ 0.705). On topic classification it
is not --- similarity collapses \textbf{0.691 $\rightarrow$ 0.361}, because that
prompt instructs the model to change the topic ``from world to sports''. A text
whose topic really changed is a different input with a different correct label, so
a prediction change on it is the classifier behaving \emph{correctly}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
& Flip rate & Meaning-preserving & Ratio \\
\midrule
AG News & 65.0\% & \textbf{3.3\%} & $19.5\times$ \\
SST-2 & 50.0\% & 15.0\% & $3.3\times$ \\
IMDb & 83.3\% & 46.7\% & $1.8\times$ \\
\midrule
\textbf{Pooled} & \textbf{66.1\%} & \textbf{21.7\%} & $\mathbf{3.05\times}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The instruction most effective at producing label flips is the
instruction to change the label.} Any prompt search that optimizes reported attack
success will find it.

\subsection*{Input validity --- understates, so the errors do not cancel}

Sentence-pair tasks need two segments. Most probes merge them and are not
well-formed inputs at all (100 of 198 in the stored corpus; for RTE under PWWS,
\textbf{0 of 31}). Malformed probes never register a flip, so leaving them in the
denominator counts them as attack \emph{failures}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Prompt & Well-formed & Unchecked & Valid only \\
\midrule
generic & 0.300 & 12.5\% & \textbf{41.7\%} \\
structured & \textbf{0.575} & 21.7\% & 37.7\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Stating the format constraint nearly doubles validity
($p=2.8\times10^{-5}$), but the model still violates an explicit format over 40\%
of the time. Label validity inflates the reported number, input validity deflates
it --- \textbf{the two errors push in opposite directions and do not cancel.}

\subsection*{Generator validity --- the red-teamer itself refuses}

The generator is safety-trained and sometimes declines: \emph{``I cannot provide a
revised text that could potentially be used to mislead or deceive\ldots''} Of 600
generations, \textbf{37 (6.2\%)} begin with a refusal, and \textbf{7 of those are
scored as successful attacks} (mean similarity 0.216 to the original). It is not
evenly spread over the conditions being compared: \textbf{11.0\%} under the generic
prompt against \textbf{0.6\%} under the task-specific one, and \textbf{17.5\%} on
RTE. Excluding refusals leaves the results above intact (118/179 vs.\ 52/170,
$p=4.2\times10^{-11}$), but a refusal and a failed attack look identical in the
output column, so the check is not optional.

\subsection*{What the filter trades}

Per-candidate filtering on all three conditions, 360 probes:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Stage & Kept & Frac. & Mean sim.\ / median ppl \\
\midrule
all probes & 360 & 1.000 & 0.648 / 33.8 \\
+ flips the classifier & 177 & 0.492 & 0.596 / 28.1 \\
+ similarity $\geq 0.75$ & 64 & 0.178 & 0.840 / 27.2 \\
+ perplexity $\leq 150$ & 55 & 0.153 & 0.834 / 22.9 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Retains \textbf{15.3\%}, and \textbf{similarity does the filtering, not
fluency}: it removes 113 of the 177 that flip, perplexity 9 more. Fluency is nearly
free --- TextFooler's own perturbations sit at median perplexity \textbf{522}
against 22.9. Underneath everything, rewriting keeps only \textbf{44.5\%} of the
attacks it is applied to: a trade, not an improvement.

\vspace{3pt}
\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}
\vspace{1pt}

\noindent\textbf{Four numbers a red-teaming report should carry.}
(1) semantic similarity beside every flip rate --- on AG News, the difference
between 65.0\% and 3.3\%; (2) the fraction of generations that are well-formed
inputs --- on pair tasks, 12.5\% vs.\ 41.7\%; (3) the generator's refusal rate per
condition --- here 11.0\% vs.\ 0.6\%; (4) the number of generations actually scored
by the target model --- without it a pipeline can report a metric computed from
something other than what it generated, and nothing will look wrong.

\begin{center}
{\large\textbf{What LLM Rewriting Costs an Adversarial Attack}}\\[1pt]
{\small Yihang Jiao \quad$\cdot$\quad yihangj3@illinois.edu \quad$\cdot$\quad August 2026
\quad$\cdot$\quad \url{https://github.com/yhj3/counterfactual-textattack}}
\end{center}
\vspace{-3pt}
\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}
\vspace{2pt}

\noindent
The 2024 study proposed a generate-and-filter pipeline: have LLaMA-2 rewrite an adversarial example
so it reads naturally, then keep only the rewrites that flip the classifier, stay semantically close,
and read fluently. This version elaborates that filter into a per-candidate test --- every rewrite
is scored on all three conditions and kept or dropped individually --- and measures what it costs.
Because rewriting is applied to attacks that \emph{already succeed}, the cost is directly
measurable: how many of those attacks survive being rewritten?

\subsection*{1. Rewriting keeps less than half the attacks}

Running each victim classifier on the rewritten text, over 32 configurations
(2 architectures $\times$ 2 attack recipes $\times$ 8 datasets):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
& AG News & IMDb & MR & SST-2 & CoLA \\
\midrule
attacks surviving the rewrite & 0.907 & 0.764 & 0.268 & 0.217 & 0.069 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Mean over the 20 single-sentence configurations: \textbf{44.5\%}. The spread is a factor
of thirteen, and it lines up with which datasets had a hand-written prompt --- so the next
experiment separates prompt from dataset.

\subsection*{2. The prompt governs the loss --- and one prompt cheats}

Both prompt variants run on every dataset, 30 successful TextFooler attacks per cell:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llccl}
\toprule
Dataset & Victim & Task-specific & Generic & $p$ (Fisher) \\
\midrule
AG News & BERT & 20/30 & 10/30 & 0.019 \\
AG News & RoBERTa & 19/30 & 8/30 & 0.009 \\
IMDb & BERT & 22/30 & 7/30 & 0.0002 \\
IMDb & RoBERTa & 28/30 & 6/30 & $<10^{-4}$ \\
SST-2 & BERT & 12/30 & 13/30 & 1.000 \\
SST-2 & RoBERTa & 18/30 & 14/30 & 0.438 \\
\midrule
\textbf{Pooled} & & \textbf{119/180} & \textbf{58/180} & $\mathbf{1.7\times10^{-10}}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent A task-specific prompt roughly doubles retention. On sentiment this is free: similarity
is unchanged (0.713 vs.\ 0.705). On AG News it is not --- similarity collapses
\textbf{0.691 $\rightarrow$ 0.361}, because that prompt instructs the model to change the topic
``from world to sports''. A text whose topic really changed is a different input with a different
correct label, not an attack. \emph{The instruction most effective at producing label flips is the
instruction to change the label}, so flip rate alone cannot be the metric.

\subsection*{3. Sentence-pair tasks fail structurally}

RTE, QNLI and MRPC need two segments. Most rewrites merge them and are not valid classifier inputs
at all --- 100 of 198 across the stored corpus; for RTE under PWWS, \textbf{0 of 31}. Adding an
explicit structure constraint to the prompt raises validity from \textbf{30.0\% to 57.5\%}
($p = 2.8\times10^{-5}$) and usable attacks from 12.5\% to 21.7\% --- but the model still violates
a stated format more than 40\% of the time.

\subsection*{4. What the filter actually trades}

The elaborated three-stage filter, applied per candidate to all 360 single-sentence rewrites:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Stage & Kept & Fraction & Mean sim.\ / median ppl \\
\midrule
all rewrites & 360 & 1.000 & 0.648 / 33.8 \\
+ flips the classifier & 177 & 0.492 & 0.596 / 28.1 \\
+ similarity $\geq 0.75$ & 64 & 0.178 & 0.840 / 27.2 \\
+ perplexity $\leq 150$ & 55 & 0.153 & 0.834 / 22.9 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The filter does what it claims --- mean similarity rises from 0.648 to 0.834 --- and keeps
\textbf{15.3\%} of generations. \textbf{Similarity does the filtering, not fluency}: it removes 113
of the 177 candidates that flip, while the perplexity bound removes 9 more. The fluency gain over the
attacks being replaced is large: TextFooler's own perturbations have median GPT-2-XL perplexity
\textbf{522}, against 22.9 here.

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{The trade, stated plainly:} LLM rewriting buys a large improvement in fluency and
pays for it in attack retention. Neither side is small, and the exchange rate is set by the prompt.
